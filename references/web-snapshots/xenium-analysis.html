How to read these slides

Two honest caveats up front

n = 1 patient, ~1–2 samples per timepoint. Clusters are annotated independently per sample, so cross-sample comparisons are semantic and hypothesis-generating — directions, not statistics.
Region ≠ contact. "Inside the tumour region" (signed distance to a majority-malignant mask) is a different question from "touching tumour cells" (neighbour enrichment). Here they disagree — and that disagreement is the headline of Act 4, not a contradiction.
Set expectations before any result.
  • n = 1, per-sample clustering → cross-sample comparisons are directions, not statistics.

Method — defining "tumour" two ways

Where is the tumour?

Every specificity test below is run under both definitions, so a finding can't be an artefact of either one:

  • Malignant clusters — cells whose annotated type is malignant (the confident clone).
  • Tumour region mask — a territory, not a cell label:
The signed-distance mask
Rasterize malignant cells on a 20 µm grid → keep bins that are majority-malignant → a 40 µm morphological close fills inter-cell gaps into one solid region → an exact Euclidean distance transform gives every cell a signed distance: negative inside, positive outside.

The region holds ~2× as many cells as the clusters (it sweeps in the stroma and immune cells trapped inside the mass). Reassuringly, the two definitions agree on which variants are allele-specific.

Two tumour definitions, every test run under both.
  • Clusters = cells called malignant (conservative).
  • Region = a territory: rasterize 20 µm → majority rule → close 40 µm gaps → signed distance.
  • Region holds ~2× the cells (sweeps in trapped stroma/immune). They agree — that's the point.

The "malignant" label, unpacked

What makes a cell malignant?

Each expression cluster is labelled by a model reading its top-20 differential markers (returning a type, a confidence, and the gene rationale). The "malignant" call rests on two recurring signatures:

  • Osteoblastic identity — RUNX2, SP7 (Osterix), SATB2, IBSP, PANX3: the osteosarcoma lineage program.
  • Proliferation & genome instability — CDK1/2, EZH2, BRCA1/2, RAD51: cycling and DNA-repair activity that normal bone cells lack.

(Cancer-testis antigens — MAGE / PAGE — reinforce the call in the one sample where they surface.) 2–4 clusters per sample, ~22–32% of cells — stable across all six independent annotations; 76% of malignant cells are called "high" confidence.

The hard part. Osteosarcoma cells are osteoblast-like — they share RUNX2 / SP7 / COL1A1 with normal bone-forming cells, so markers alone can't perfectly separate malignant from normal osteoblasts. The proliferation / genome-instability layer is what tips the call — normal osteoblasts aren't cycling.
An independent check. The patient's own somatic ALT alleles — never used to make this call — are detected ~4–5× more often in the malignant clusters than anywhere else (DYNC1H1 up to 9×). The expression-defined label and the genotype-defined clone coincide.

Too many? normal osteoblasts or doublets could inflate it (the medium-confidence clusters, mostly in C3). Too few? dedifferentiated tumour cells that dropped the osteoblastic program may hide among stroma. We hedge by running every test under both the cluster and region definitions.

Be candid — this label carries the analysis.
  • A model reads each cluster's top-20 DE markers; no ground truth.
  • Hard part: osteosarcoma cells look like osteoblasts (RUNX2/SP7/COL1A1); proliferation markers (CDK1/EZH2/BRCA/RAD51) tip the call.
  • Reassurance: the patient's own ALT alleles (not used to label) are 4–5× enriched in these clusters. ~22–32% of cells, stable across all 6.

The interpretation trap

Detection isn't confirmation

  • All 33 variants are detected — but detection is dominated by expression: H1-2 (a histone) fires in 134k cells, CUL9 in 308.
  • Allele probes are low-efficiency — they capture only ~1–39% (median 9%) of the gene's plain expression probe. Counts are a pseudo-VAF, not a true allele fraction.
"ALT enriched in tumour" is not enough. If a gene is simply more expressed in tumour, its WT probe is enriched too. ATRX: ALT 2.1× enriched (p≈10⁻²⁰³) — but WT equally so, and pseudo-VAF identical in tumour vs rest (0.39 vs 0.37). Not allele-specific.
The honest test for somatic specificity
ALT enriched beyond WT and pseudo-VAF higher in tumour than in the rest — applied per sample, under both tumour definitions. The WT probe is the built-in control (a germline allele should sit everywhere the gene is expressed).
Mind the noise floor. Many allele probes fire near the negative-control background. We gate on signal-to-control ≥ 2×; 10 of 33 fall below it (e.g. MAP2, ABI3BP) and are flagged low-signal no matter how clean their ratio looks. Dropout (~1 transcript per cell) likewise makes single-cell co-detection an undercount — so we lean on enrichment and allele balance, not raw counts.
Two interpretation traps.
  • Detection tracks expression, not mutation — counts are a pseudo-VAF (probes capture ~1–39%).
  • "ALT up in tumour" is confounded: if the gene is just more expressed, WT is up too (= ATRX).
  • Honest test: ALT beyond WT + signal-to-control ≥ 2×. 10/33 sit at the noise floor (MAP2) — not called.

What "~0.9 ALT" actually counts

The pseudo-VAF, in words
Among the ~1,950 tumour cells that carry either allele, 1,830 (94%) carry ALT and 118 (6%) carry WT → pseudo-VAF 0.94. Outside the tumour it's 0.63. That tumour-vs-rest gap is the somatic signal.
So it's a ratio over a thin slice. The direction (ALT concentrates in tumour) is rock-solid — it rests on all 34k tumour vs 106k non-tumour cells, where uniform background can't fake a contrast. The exact value (0.94) is an estimate from ~1,950 informative cells, so we read it as "ALT-dominant," not a precise allele fraction.

Allelic state

Zygosity & loss of heterozygosity

Among cells expressing the gene, the split of WT-only / ALT-only / both reveals the allelic state:

  • ALT-dominant / LOH — DYNC1H1 (84% ALT-only, s/c 27×) and ZNF436 (tumour VAF 0.94, WT depleted in tumour): the reference allele is largely gone.
  • Reference retained — SLC25A12 still carries WT; and the highly-expressed histone H1-2 catches both alleles in 21% of cells (dropout in reverse).

Dropout caveat. Except for highly-expressed genes, "both" cells are rare (~1 transcript/cell), so co-detection under-reads true heterozygosity. The robust LOH evidence is ALT dominance + WT depletion in tumour, not the both-fraction. (All six shown clear the signal-to-control gate.)

A therapeutic transgene, read in situ

The panel sees the oncolytic virus arrive

Two panel probes target AdAPT-001 (EpicentRx) — a replication-competent type-5 adenovirus armed with a TGF-β trap transgene.[9] epicentrx-left-right detects the inserted construct; epicentrx-left-ref is its paired reference. The patient received 7 intratumoral doses after T1.

Does the construct probe track the dosing?
Exactly. It sits below the negative-control noise floor at T0 and T1 (signal-to-control 0.07–0.26), jumps to 14× background at T2, and surges to 520–740× at T3. The reference probe barely moves (≤1.2 until a modest 7–10× at T3) — so the construct/reference ratio climbs ~0.2 → 12 → ~75.
Why it explodes. The virus is selectively lytic in cancer cells, and its genome — including the trap transgene — is copy-amplified by viral replication inside infected tumour cells.[9] We are watching the therapy replicate in the tissue.
Which cells carry it? The lysis hides the answer. As the construct surges, it becomes overwhelmingly extracellular — the fraction inside segmented cells falls from ~90% (at the noise floor) to just 8% at T3, and ~90% of that in-cell remainder lands in unsegmentable lysing-cell / doublet clusters (~2 copies/cell). That is the fingerprint of active oncolysis, not a clean cell-type tag. At T2 (earlier, 27% still in-cell) the signal spreads across TAM, malignant and CD8 cells. Consistent with replication in tumour, but segmentation can't pin it to one intact lineage.
The therapy, read in situ.

  • Construct probe = textbook pharmacodynamic readout: below noise floor T0/T1, 14× at T2, 520–740× at T3; reference flat.
  • Steep because the virus copy-amplifies its transgene as it replicates in tumour.

The headline — region vs contact

Two different questions

"Inside" can mean two things

In the tumour region?
Signed distance to a majority-malignant mask. Negative = inside the tumour mass (which includes stromal/immune pockets).
Touching tumour cells?
Neighbourhood enrichment — is a cell type adjacent to malignant cells more or less than chance?

A T cell can be deep inside the region yet never touch a malignant cell. Watch these two answers diverge.

Are they armed?

Cytotoxic potential

Searching for TLS — a cautious null

Method — gene-set spatial coherence

Do a program's genes light up together?

For one gene set (e.g. IFN-γ response, 32 measured genes):

  • Lay a 100 µm quadrat grid over the slide — each gene becomes a vector of per-quadrat transcript counts.
  • Take the Spearman correlation of every gene pair across quadrats — do gene A and gene B light up the same regions?
  • Coherence = the mean of those pairwise correlations.
What it captures
Spatial co-localization of a program's genes — not their expression level. High → the genes concentrate in shared neighbourhoods; ≈0 → scattered independently.
Why a z-score, not the raw number
Compare the set's coherence to a null of random panel-gene sets of the same size (thousands of random 32-gene draws): z = (observed − null mean) / null SD. This controls for set size and the slide's overall structure.
Worked example. IFN-γ response — coherence ≈ 0.52, z ≈ 5.4 in a T3 sample: its 32 genes co-localize ~5 SD more tightly than a random 32-gene set. The next slide plots the mean z across the 8 immune sets, per sample.

Normalized to random genes in the same tissue, the z isolates immune organization from whatever global structure a given section happens to have.

Coherence, explained.
  • Do a program's genes light up in the SAME places (not how highly)?
  • Grid the slide (100 µm) → correlate every gene pair across squares → average = coherence.
  • z = SD above random same-size gene sets (controls for set size + global tissue structure).
  • Note: z rises monotonically; raw coherence peaks at T2 (globally blocky) — the z divides that out.

Synthesis (hypothesis)

A working model

Across this patient's course, the tumour stays excluded at the malignant-cell border throughout. Early on it is myeloid-dominated and quiet; over time CD8 T cells accumulate and penetrate the tumour mass, but engage the SPP1⁺ macrophage stroma at interfaces rather than the cancer cells — while Tregs reach the core and immune programs become increasingly organized. A cytotoxic-but-checkpointed, interface-confined response held off by myeloid + Treg gatekeeping.

A model to test — not a conclusion. Directly actionable hypotheses: PD-1/CTLA4 blockade (TCF7⁺ progenitor CD8 present), SPP1/myeloid targeting, and a TLS search.
